The workbench

How a Camera Becomes a Lamp

It takes longer than you’d think. Here’s what happens to every camera that comes through the garage door.

developer

Find it

A box camera, a reflex, an old folding bellows or an 8mm movie camera that stopped taking pictures a long time ago.

stop

Leave it be

We don’t sand off the dents or swap the glass. The wear is the part you can’t fake.

fixer

Wire it

Then it’s wired by hand and tested on the bench, until it’s something you’d use, not just look at.

wash

Number it

Last, it gets its number stamped and a tag saying what camera it used to be. Then it goes on the line.

Questions at the counter

Vintage Camera Lighting, Answered

What is a vintage camera lamp?

A working lamp built from a real vintage camera. We take box cameras, twin-lens reflexes, folding bellows and 8mm movie cameras whose picture-taking days are done, keep the original glass, badges and honest wear, and convert each one by hand into a lamp for everyday use. Read our camera lamp guide.

Are the cameras real?

Yes. Every lamp starts as a real camera that once took real pictures. The body, the glass and the badges are all its own. No reproductions.

Can you turn my own camera into a lamp?

Yes. Send us a camera you already own, a family heirloom included, or ask us to find one for you. Fill out the commission envelope and we write back with a price and a plan. No payment is due until you say yes.

Is every lamp one of a kind?

Yes. No two cameras lived the same life, so no two lamps come out the same. Each one is numbered and stamped, and wears a tag naming the camera it used to be.

Where are the lamps made?

In our small family-run garage workshop in St. Louis, Missouri. Every lamp is made by hand and shipped from St. Louis.

Do you deliver?

Yes. Delivery is free for local orders around St. Louis, and we bring the lamp to you ourselves. Lamps headed farther afield ship from our St. Louis workshop.

Where can I buy a vintage camera lamp?

Right here in the shop, or from our eBay store. Follow @luminousartifacts on Instagram to see new lamps as they come off the bench.
